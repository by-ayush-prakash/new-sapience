Key characteristics

What follows from the architecture, rather than from guardrails bolted on afterwards.

Sapiens and Language

Comprehension, not text

Where the mission requires continuous or occasional humans-in-the-loop, sapiens can communicate in natural language. Sapiens do not process text, they comprehend language. They do not generate text, they articulate concepts.

The sapiens capacity to comprehend language is made possible by a commonsense world model that is available in even the smallest applications.

Deterministic, Explainable Logic

Same input, same output

Every time, without exception. Sapiens reason from a cognitive world model of how things actually work. There is no randomness, no variance, no second-guessing of work, no surprises.

Every step of a sapiens’ reasoning is visible and open to inspection. Not a summary. The full chain of thought, reviewable at any point by anyone who needs to verify it.

Security

Air-gap capable

A sapiens can be deployed as a stand-alone device. No internet connection, no cloud, no data center required. This includes environments that can be “air-gapped” from the outside world, reducing vulnerabilities.

Customers own their proprietary model, not just system output. Nothing has to phone home.

Knows Its Own Boundary

Responds flexibly

A sapiens makes decisions based on what it knows. But it also recognizes when it encounters the boundary of its knowledge and can respond flexibly to find the missing information or obtain assistance.

This isn’t

Machine Learning

Machine Learning uses statistical patterns to simulate intelligence the way a video of fire simulates fire: it’s a realistic likeness, but the video is not on fire. What’s missing is precisely what’s essential.

If you want rationally competent machines, you don’t need a bigger, better camera; you need a different kind of instrument.

This isn’t

Symbolic AI

Symbolic AI applications like expert systems or semantic network ontologies are based on collections of rules with no principled account of how knowledge is structured. This is why they are brittle and difficult to scale beyond small applications: a heap of rules is not a model of a domain.

A sapiens holds a world model grounded in a robust epistemology; it’s a dynamic map of how the domain is and behaves rather than a list of heuristics that only work inside it, under static conditions.
It is

Synthetic Intelligence

New Sapience has developed a platform for scalable, deterministic, world-model-based applications in which knowledge is achieved as non-symbolic, directed graph structures.

Our IDE enables commonsense and expert knowledge derived from the customer’s domain and requirements to be reconstructed in computer memory as synthetic knowledge.

Synthetic Intelligence is real knowledge but not contained in a natural mind — in the same way a synthetic diamond is a real diamond but not created by natural processes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1What is a world model?+
It is a model that understands the dynamics of the real world, including physics and spatial properties. It is an understanding of the world as it really exists. It can be as big or as small as needed. For example, the world model for a satellite is everything associated with that satellite. That satellite’s world model is thorough and complete, but simple and concise. No room for error.
Q2What is synthetic knowledge?+
To be clear, synthetic knowledge is not a symbolic representation of knowledge but is knowledge itself. It is derived from information, patterns, and insights generated artificially by models, rather than directly from real-world observation. It is human knowledge transformed into synthetic form.
Q3What does deterministic mean?+
It is a system, process, or philosophy where the outcome is entirely determined by initial conditions and inputs, leaving no room for randomness or chance.
Q4What does probabilistic mean?+
It is a system, process, or philosophy based on or adapted to a theory of probability. It is subject to or involves chance variation.
Q5What does hallucination mean?+
This term has been applied to large language model AI systems that provide a false, inaccurate or nonsensical response ungrounded in truth or facts.
Q6What is a high-stakes industry?+
It is a sector where the consequences of a mistake, failure, or poor decision could pose significant repercussions such as banking, healthcare, and the legal profession. Included in high-stakes industries are mission-critical systems.
Q7What does mission-critical mean?+
Mission-critical systems are vital to the functioning of a civilized society and for the public good, such as national security, finance, and energy production & distribution. Disruptions in any of these could lead to catastrophic financial, public safety or human losses.
Q8What does zero-defect mean?+
It is the concept of operations or quality management focused on preventing errors. Defects are unacceptable.
Q9What does air-gapped mean?+
A device or system that is physically isolated from outside networks: no internet, no cloud, no data centers. It is not vulnerable to external bad actors.
Q10What is an edge device?+
It is a hardware component located at the boundary of a network—close to data sources or users—that collects, processes, and analyzes data locally rather than sending it to a centralized cloud or data center.
Q11How can sapiens be audited?+
Auditing means being able to trace back how any given output or behavior was determined by the system. Both the knowledge that sapiens contain and the program that reasons about it are written and readable by humans. Knowledge that supports specific applications is certified and owned by the customer.
